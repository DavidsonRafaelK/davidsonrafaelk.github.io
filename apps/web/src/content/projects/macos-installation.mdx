My laptop was slow. The kind of slow where opening a second app is a decision
you think about first.

Then a relative lent me a MacBook for one day and told me to do whatever I
wanted with it. I spent the whole day just using it. What stuck with me was not
any particular feature. Apps opened with no delay at all, and moving between
them cost nothing. After a year of waiting for windows to finish drawing
themselves, it felt like a different category of machine.

I gave it back the next morning and started wondering whether I could get macOS
running on my own hardware, either in a VM or straight on the metal.

## Everything failed in the same place

Most guides I found did not work. Different steps, different tools, same
ending: bootloop. The installer starts, the Apple logo shows up, the machine
restarts. Again and again.

So I stopped working through tutorials one at a time and lined all of them up
next to each other instead, along with whatever else I could dig up, looking for
what they had in common.

It came down to the CPU. Mine is an AMD Ryzen. Macs run Intel, and macOS is
built expecting Intel. The two are different enough that the kernel gives up
early.

## Lying to the kernel

That changed the question. Instead of "how do I install macOS on AMD", it became
"how do I make macOS believe this is not an AMD chip".

That is what the CPUID spoof does. A script rewrites the CPU identity the kernel
reads at boot, so macOS sees an Intel i7 instead of a Ryzen. Once that was in
place the bootloop stopped and the installer ran all the way through.

Everything after that was ordinary. Partitioning, install, first boot, signing
in with an Apple account. No tricks needed, it just worked.

It was also unbelievably slow, because my laptop was still my laptop.

## What's in the guide

The requirements section does most of the work. Before anything else it tells
you what your machine needs:

- A CPU with AVX2, because macOS 13 and up require it
- Virtualization turned on in BIOS, AMD-V or Intel VT-x
- 8 GB of RAM minimum, 16 GB if you want a good time
- 70 GB of disk minimum, 200 GB recommended

It also notes that [VMware Workstation Pro](https://access.broadcom.com/) went
free for personal and commercial use in May 2024, which makes a lot of the
licensing advice still floating around outdated.

The AMD warning is the part most guides bury: turn off Hyper-V and Windows
Memory Integrity first. If you don't, the VM fails in a way that gives you no
hint about why.

After that it walks through pulling VMware Workstation Pro from the Broadcom
portal, finding a macOS Sequoia 15 image on [Olarila](https://www.olarila.com/),
and running the [DrDonk unlocker](https://github.com/DrDonk/unlocker) that makes
macOS show up as a guest option at all, then the install itself and a
troubleshooting section for when it still refuses to boot.

## What I still haven't fixed

3D acceleration. The VM has no GPU acceleration, so the entire desktop is drawn
on the CPU. It stays heavy even on a Windows laptop with a dedicated GPU,
because the GPU is not the part doing the work.

I have not found a fix, and I am not convinced a clean one exists. So the guide
gets you a working macOS, just not a fast one.
